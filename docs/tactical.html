Overview

Three simulation modules exploring the aerodynamics and continuum mechanics of team formations. Players are modelled as bluff bodies or Gaussian influence fields, revealing drafting benefits, defensive stress concentrations, and passing lane permeability.

1. Formation Aerodynamics

Players modelled as rectangular bluff bodies in a 10 m/s crossflow. Three configurations:

Case 1
Isolated winger — baseline drag
Case 2
Midfield press wall — vorticity amplification
Case 3
Overlapping fullback — 26.1% drag reduction
Wake interaction across three formation configurations

Key Result: Wake Shielding

In Case 3, the second player sits in the wake of the first. The drag on the downstream player drops by 26.1% — a direct analogue to cycling peloton drafting and Formula 1 slipstreaming.

2. Tactical Stress Fields (7v7)

Players modelled as anisotropic, velocity-stretched Gaussian influence fields. Attackers advance left-to-right while defenders drop deeper over 60 eased frames.

Stress field evolution — red = attacking advantage, blue = defensive advantage

The stress field is:

$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}, t) = \tanh\left(\frac{A(\mathbf{x}, t) - D(\mathbf{x}, t)}{3.0}\right) $$
(1) Tactical stress as a two-phase interface

where $A$ and $D$ are cumulative attacker/defender influence fields. Yield points are detected as 8-neighbour local maxima where $\sigma > 0.4$.

Static stress field — zero-stress contour (white) marks the equilibrium boundary
