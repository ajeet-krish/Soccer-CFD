# Overview

Six simulation modules exploring the aerodynamics and continuum mechanics of team formations. Players are modelled as bluff bodies, Gaussian influence fields, and dynamic sliding arrays — revealing drafting benefits, defensive stress concentrations, passing lane permeability, and the fluid-structure analogy of tactical movement.

# 1. Formation Aerodynamics

Players modelled as rectangular bluff bodies in a 10 m/s crossflow. Three configurations:

Case 3: Overlapping Fullback (Tandem Drag Reduction)

Two players aligned streamwise: a fullback overlapping a winger. The downstream player enters the upstream wake and experiences 26.1% drag reduction — the drafting effect familiar from cycling and motorsport, now quantified for soccer.

Tactical insight: A 26.1% drag reduction means the overlapping player expends significantly less energy to maintain sprint speed. This is the fluid-dynamic basis for why overlapping runs are so effective in the final third — the attacking player arrives at the byline with more energy reserved for the cross.

# 2. Continuum Stress Fields

7v7 tactical formations modelled as two-phase fluid interfaces. Defenders are compressive stresses; attackers are tensile stresses. The scalar stress field $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ reveals structural weak points:

σ > 0.4
Yield point — defensive breach
σ < -0.3
Compressive stability — secure block
|σ| < 0.1
Neutral zone — transition area
Engineering analogy: Just as stress concentrations in a loaded beam predict crack initiation sites, stress concentrations in a formation predict defensive breaches. A yield point (σ > 0.4) is the fluid-dynamic equivalent of a defensive line breaking — the attacker has found a path through the structure.

# 3. Darcy Permeability (Passing Lanes)

11v11 defensive influence modelled as porous media. Each defender contributes an anisotropic Gaussian influence field $\phi(\mathbf{x})$. The cumulative field defines regions of high defensive density (impermeable) and low density (permeable passing lanes).

# 4. Ball Movement Strategies

Three ball movement modes analysed through the lens of continuum transport:

Short passing triangles 3-player passing networks advancing through defensive zones Porous flow through granular media
Switch of play Long diagonal ball to opposite flank Pressure wave through compressible medium

# 5. Lateral Shifting Mechanics

Analysis of defensive blocks sliding as coherent units. The entire back line shifts laterally in response to ball movement, maintaining spacing and zonal coverage.

# 6. Attack / Defence Transitions

The moment the ball is lost: formation transitions from offensive shape (tensile, dispersed) to defensive shape (compressive, compact). Modelled as a phase change in the stress field over a characteristic reaction time $\tau \approx 2$–3 seconds.
