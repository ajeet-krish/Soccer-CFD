Tactical Positioning

Overview

Seven simulation modules exploring the aerodynamics and continuum mechanics of team formations. Players are modelled as bluff bodies, Gaussian influence fields, and dynamic sliding arrays: revealing drafting benefits, defensive stress concentrations, passing lane permeability, and the fluid-structure analogy of tactical movement.

Formation Pressure Maps

Every formation creates a distinct defensive pressure field across the pitch. By modelling each defender as a Gaussian influence source and superimposing their contributions, the resulting scalar field $p(\mathbf{x})$ reveals exactly where space exists and where it is congested. This is the same mathematical framework used in CFD: instead of modeling pressure perturbations around bluff bodies in a flow, each player generates a zone of influence that attackers must navigate through, and the superposition principle (summing individual contributions to obtain the total field) is identical to how CFD solves flow fields from boundary conditions.

A Gaussian influence field is a bell-shaped function $e^{-r^2 / (2\sigma^2)}$ centered on each player, where $r$ is distance from the player and $\sigma$ controls the radius of influence. The choice of Gaussian is not arbitrary, it mirrors how perturbations decay in potential flow theory. A centre-back with $\sigma = 4.0$ exerts wide zone control spanning the penalty area; a winger with $\sigma = 2.5$ has a narrow, concentrated influence reflecting their position-specific role. Mathematically, the total pressure at any point is the sum of all 11 Gaussians, which is a 2D kernel density estimate of the defending team's spatial structure.

The pitch is divided into five vertical lanes, a standard framework in modern soccer analysis:

Lane Tactical role
Left Wing Traditional touchline channel. Primary territory for wide midfielders and overlapping fullbacks.
Left Half-Space The half-space: between the penalty box edge and the central channel. This is where creative playmakers operate, exploiting diagonal passing angles that split centre-backs and fullbacks.
Center The central corridor. Highest density of players in most formations. Dominated by centre-backs, defensive midfielders, and the opposition striker.
Right Half-Space Mirror of left half-space. Attacking midfielders drifting into this zone can create 3v2 overloads against the fullback-centre-back gap.
Right Wing Mirror of left wing. Outlet for switches of play and isolation 1v1 situations.

The half-spaces are tactically decisive because passing angles through them bypass the central density wall. Each formation lane analysis below reports the average defensive pressure per lane as a percentage of the maximum lane average across all formations, providing a direct quantitative comparison of structural strengths and weaknesses.

Formation Aerodynamics

In soccer, overlapping runs are a fundamental attacking tactic: the fullback sprints past the winger on the outside, receiving the ball in space behind the defence. The physics behind its effectiveness has a clear fluid-dynamic explanation — tandem drag reduction.

Two players aligned streamwise form a tandem bluff-body configuration. The downstream player (the overlapping fullback) enters the low-pressure wake of the upstream player (the winger). Within this wake, the oncoming flow speed is reduced and the dynamic pressure head is lower, producing a 26.1% drag reduction for the trailing player. This is exactly the drafting effect familiar from cycling time-trials and Formula 1 slipstreaming, where a following vehicle experiences significantly less aerodynamic resistance.

The flow was modelled using $\Phi$Flow with two rectangular bluff bodies (width $w = 0.3$ m, height $h = 1.8$ m) at $Re = 2 \times 10^5$, spaced one body-length apart in the streamwise direction.

Tactical insight: A 26.1% drag reduction means the overlapping player expends significantly less energy to maintain sprint speed. This is the fluid-dynamic basis for why overlapping runs are so effective in the final third: the attacking player arrives at the byline with more energy reserved for the cross.
