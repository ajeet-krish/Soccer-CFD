# Governing Equations

Navier-Stokes

All CFD simulations in this project (both ΦFlow and SU2) solve the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

$$ \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0 $$ $$ \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla) \mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho} \nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} $$
(1) Conservation of mass and momentum

where $\mathbf{u}$ is the velocity field, $p$ is pressure, $\rho$ is density, and $\nu$ is kinematic viscosity.

ΦFlow approach: Semi-Lagrangian advection + iterative CG pressure solve on a staggered MAC grid. Fast and differentiable — ideal for parametric sweeps.
SU2 approach: Finite-volume RANS with Menter SST $k$-$\omega$ turbulence model. Unstructured meshes resolve boundary layers for accurate skin friction and separation points.

Reynolds Number

The governing dimensionless parameter for all flows considered:

$$ Re = \frac{\rho U L}{\mu} = \frac{U L}{\nu} $$
(2) Ratio of inertial to viscous forces

where $U$ is the freestream velocity, $L$ is the characteristic length (ball diameter or cylinder diameter), and $\mu$ is dynamic viscosity.

Drag Crisis

At $Re \approx 2 \times 10^5$, a sphere experiences a sudden drop in drag coefficient $C_d$ from $\sim 0.5$ to $\sim 0.1$ — the drag crisis. This occurs when the boundary layer transitions from laminar to turbulent, moving the separation point aft and reducing the pressure drag.

Magnus Effect

A rotating body in a crossflow experiences a lift force perpendicular to the freestream:

$$ F_L = C_L \cdot \frac{1}{2} \rho A U^2 $$
(3) Magnus lift force

The spin creates an asymmetric boundary layer — faster flow on one side reduces pressure (Bernoulli), slower flow on the other increases pressure. The resulting pressure differential deflects the body's trajectory.

Continuum Mechanics Analogy

Team formations are modelled as a stress field $\sigma$:

$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \tanh\left(\frac{A(\mathbf{x}) - D(\mathbf{x})}{s}\right) $$
(4) Tactical stress field

where $A$ and $D$ are attacker and defender influence fields (anisotropic Gaussians), and $s$ is a smoothing parameter. Yield points are local maxima where $\sigma > 0.4$, indicating structural breaches in the defensive block.

Darcy Permeability

Passing lane availability is quantified using a porous media analogy:

$$ \kappa = \frac{\text{Area where } \phi(\mathbf{x}) < \text{threshold}}{\text{Total defensive half area}} $$
(5) Defensive permeability

where $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ is the cumulative defensive influence. Lower $\kappa$ means a more compact, less permissive defensive shape.
